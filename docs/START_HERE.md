# Fundraiser Planner volunteer guide

Use the club’s own quote, receipts and supporter responses to decide whether an event is worth the money and volunteer time. The planner works in a browser and saves on the device you use.

## Start with one fundraiser

1. Open the planner. You can use the [website](https://williamalseth10.github.io/fundraiser-planner/) or open the downloaded **Fundraiser Planner.html** file in your browser.
2. On **Home**, choose the fundraiser you’re considering and select **Start a new plan**. If you are still choosing, choose **Help me choose a fundraiser** and select what the club already has, such as donated items, interested golfers or a supplier for preorders. Each suggestion explains the match and gives you a first question to check. Give it a name and a fundraising goal. To compare a second idea, add it under **Options**.
3. Choose **Next** to work through your figures. You can return to a step to update it as you learn more.
4. Choose **Results** to see what the club would keep at low, likely and high turnout. Choose **Full analysis** for upfront cash, cancellation exposure, break-even and the items to verify before signing.

An existing plan also offers **Find another fundraiser to compare** on Home. When you return, choose **Continue my plan** on Home to pick up at your last planning step or **See my results** to open the decision brief. Choose **Open a backup file** to work with a plan you downloaded.

## What to enter

| Gather | Enter |
| --- | --- |
| Written venue, course or supplier quote | Prices, capacity, minimum headcount or bill, fees, tax and service charges. Check “in writing” only once you have the quote. |
| Deposit and cancellation terms | Deposit amount, due date, refund rule, quote expiry, final headcount deadline and the amount owed if you cancel. Check dated or tiered cancellation terms against the contract. |
| Prices and costs | The price you would put on the order form, any group price, other expenses, and which bills must be paid before the event. |
| Sponsorship and admissions | Record collected, promised-in-writing and hoped-for sponsorship separately. Enter actual paid registrations and receipts from the club’s books. Record donated items against the specific cost they replace. |
| Supporter interest | Use the survey step to copy a question with your actual offer. Enter aggregate responses, including named yes, anonymous yes and maybe. Keep names and contact details out of the planner. |
| Turnout, time and approval | Choose low, likely and high turnout. Note the evidence for those figures, the hours the event needs, the hours available and who can sign. |

For a **deposit**, check two things in the quote. Does it count toward the final bill? Can it be refunded? A positive deposit with an unanswered term leaves the overall choice open. A confirmed $0 deposit needs neither answer.

For an **auction**, enter admission as the ticket price. For each scenario, estimate the winning-bid or raffle proceeds the club expects after selling fees. Enter purchased items, flat fees and other expenses once under costs. Compare your bid estimate with the amount needed to break even or reach the fundraising goal.

## Read and share the decision

- **Comparison net at likely turnout** is the figure the board uses to rank qualifying events. It includes ticket or product sales, collected or written sponsorship, and the cost cushion from your low case.
- **Low, likely and high scenario net** show what the club would keep with each scenario’s income and costs. If the likely scenario differs from the comparison, the brief explains which amounts changed. Paid registrations and current named yes responses show how much support there is for the turnout assumption.
- **Firm money** includes ticket or sales revenue at that turnout plus sponsorship collected or promised in writing. **Cash in hand** includes money actually received. Estimated auction bids and other conditional income can change the projected proceeds.
- In **Full analysis → Ready to commit?**, enter the smallest amount worth raising, maximum acceptable loss, available volunteer hours and the decide-by date. A blank limit means it still needs a board decision. Read outstanding items alongside any projected surplus.
- **What would need to change?** lets you try another price or turnout. Applying a price changes the offer. Keep a past survey round if you need its record and ask supporters about the new terms.
- Choose **Copy decision brief** or **Print brief** for a meeting. **Full analysis → Copy board summary** includes the detailed figures and supporting evidence.
- The event recommendation uses the options you have actually priced. It checks turnout evidence, the quote, costs, cash needed up front and the board’s loss and staffing limits. An incomplete comparison stays open. Follow the linked next step to the entry that needs attention. Review the reason with the board before choosing an option.

Under **Questions for the board**, compare regular tee times with a shotgun start at the same paid golfer count and recorded receipts. The planner shows additional shotgun-specific sponsorship needed to match the tee-time return and, separately, to reach the board minimum. Check when that money would arrive against the deposit deadline. For an auction, see what happens to likely net if the largest donated lot already included in the budget falls through. Read the remaining surplus or shortfall against the club minimum with the assumptions about other bids, fees and turnout.

## Use the club’s research

Keep sources for audience counts and local prices in **Research**. For golf, open **Compare golf registrations** and enter a point in time from a past event. For example, if the club is six weeks out today, compare today’s paid golfers with paid golfers six weeks before a comparable event. Record changes in price, course or package. The old event’s final turnout gives context for the decision.

For auctions, open **Estimate auction lots**. Enter similar items’ winning bids or confirmed unsold results, selling fees and the source. Use the count of comparable results to judge how much evidence you have. If an item is already part of the likely auction total, mark it once. Enter the club’s chosen total on **Scenarios**.

## Find out how close the estimates were

1. Before committing, open **Research → Past club results**. Enter the scheduled future date and the board’s independent net estimate from its own budget. If a comparable past event has confirmed final accounts, select it. Check the pre-decision box, then **Save current likely estimate**. The planner keeps that day’s turnout, likely net and four income and cost amounts.
2. After the event, enter actual turnout and the source of the final accounts. Enter the final net from the ledger, the four actual amounts, or both. The categories are admissions or product sales, cash sponsorship, other proceeds including net auction bids, and event costs. A positive cost difference means the club spent more than projected. If the amounts disagree with the ledger, reconcile them and then **Confirm final accounts**.
3. Read **How close were our forecasts?** for the average dollar miss and the number of times the planner estimated too high. The three-way comparison uses only completed events where the planner, the board and a prior comparable all had figures recorded before the decision. A handful of events is a starting point for review. To keep learning across plans, download a backup and choose **Bring completed club results from another plan backup** in the new plan.

You can keep the other options the board considered. Finalize the accounts only for the event you actually held. If two options have final accounts for the same event name and date, reconcile those records before using the accuracy report.

## Save, transfer or restore a plan

Plans save in this browser on this device. The website and downloaded HTML file keep separate saved plans.

- **Download backup file** in **Save & backup** keeps a copy you control. Download after important edits, before moving devices or clearing site data, and before leaving a private browsing session.
- **Open a backup file** reads that copy on another device or in another version of the planner.
- Starting a new plan or opening a backup first keeps the current saved plan as the previous plan. **Restore previous plan** swaps it back. Only one previous plan is kept in the browser. Use separate downloaded files for older plans.
- If a red **Not saved** warning appears, use **Download backup file** in that warning to keep your latest changes. If the browser cannot keep a required backup, the planner stops the replacement.
